Bill Gurley has a talk, *Runnin' Down a Dream*, about how people end up doing work they love: find the thing you can't stop thinking about, learn it obsessively, and put yourself in the room where it happens. I've listened to it more than once. This site is my version of that, with one twist: I'm not writing it from the finish line. I'm writing it from the start.

## Where I am

I'm Emilio. I grew up in Villahermosa, Mexico, and finished Computer Systems Engineering at UDLAP in 2026. Since August 2026 I've been working at Club León as a Data Analyst & Software Development Intern, building tools that help scouts evaluate and compare players. Before that I did research on AI and web scraping at INRS in Montreal through Mitacs, built a chatbot and a file converter for a swimming event, and made Power BI dashboards for a company's sales team.

In February 2027 I move to Valencia, Spain, for a master's.

## The honest part

A master's abroad costs money, and I want to pay for my life there with my own work. So I freelance: I help people finish software they started, build dashboards, automate boring work with Python, and set up chatbots. I'm not going to pretend that's a grand startup vision. It's how I pay rent while I learn, and I'd rather say that plainly.

But I also want the freelance work to compound into something. Every project teaches me how a real business runs, where it loses time and where data could help. That's the part I care about most.

## What I'll write here

- **What I'm doing and how.** Projects, tools, what worked, what didn't.
- **Business analyses.** I want to get better at understanding businesses: how they make money, why they win, where they're fragile. Writing is how I'll practice. These will be my own reasoning, with sources, and I'll say when I'm guessing.
- **Progress, in public.** Including the parts that don't go well.

## Why in public

Because it keeps me honest. If I say I'm going to do something, it's written down. And because the best opportunities I've had came from people seeing what I'd actually built, not from a CV.

*— Emilio*
